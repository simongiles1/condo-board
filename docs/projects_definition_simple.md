### How Alex Organizes Building Work: Tasks, Projects, and Sub-Projects

Imagine we’ve hired an executive assistant—let's call him Alex—and asked him to go through all 7,000 emails in our building’s history. Alex’s job is to keep our records organized so we never lose track of money, contractor work, or major building repairs.

When Alex reads an email, he uses a strict set of rules to decide where the information belongs: is it just a **Quick Task**, a **Major Building Project**, or a **Sub-Project** within a larger initiative?

---

### The Rules Alex Follows

#### 1. What is a "Task" (Service Call / Minor Fix)?

A **Task** is a routine, single-step job or a quick operational to-do. It is usually handled by one contractor, resolved quickly, and paid with a standard operational invoice.

* **Alex’s Rule:** "If a contractor comes in, fixes a specific issue in a day or two, sends an invoice, and leaves, it is a **Task**."
* **Examples:**
* Re-lubricating a squeaky garage door track.
* Fixing a broken light fixture in the 3rd-floor hallway.
* Re-keying a lock for a resident amenity room.



---

#### 2. What is a "Major Project"?

A **Major Project** is a significant building initiative. It is never just a quick repair—it involves planning, significant money, multiple quotes or board approvals, and spans weeks or months.

* **Alex’s Rule:** A job becomes a **Major Project** if it meets **at least two** of these criteria:
1. **Multiple Steps / Multiple Parties:** Requires board proposals, formal quotes from competing vendors, engineering reviews, or multi-stage work.
2. **Capital Expenditure (CapEx):** It goes beyond routine budget maintenance and involves major building reserves or large financial spends.
3. **Distinct Lifecycle:** It goes through clear stages: investigation $\rightarrow$ board approval $\rightarrow$ work execution $\rightarrow$ final inspection/sign-off.


* **Examples:**
* Re-roofing the entire building.
* Overhauling the Domestic Hot Water Boiler System.
* Hallway Refurbishment (new carpet, wallpaper, and lighting across all floors).



---

#### 3. What is a "Sub-Project"?

Sometimes a Major Project is so big that it contains separate, major phases that need their own budget tracking, specific vendor contracts, or distinct timelines.

Instead of cluttering the main project folder or treating these phases as completely separate, unrelated jobs, Alex files them as **Sub-Projects** attached directly beneath the Main Project umbrella.

* **Alex’s Rule:** "If an initiative is a major effort in its own right, but it only exists because of a broader, overarching initiative, it is a **Sub-Project**."
* **Why this matters (without technical jargon):** It allows us to look at the "Big Picture" (e.g., total cost to renovate the garage) while still letting us zoom in on the specific details of one component (e.g., just the garage door replacement contract).

### Real Condo Examples: How Work Gets Categorized

Here is how Alex handles real-world scenarios when reading through the email history:

#### Example A: The Garage Door Incidents

* **Scenario 1 (The Task):** A delivery truck hits the visitor garage door arm, bending it. A technician comes out the next morning, straightens the arm, and bills $250.
* **Alex’s Decision:** **Task.** It's a single-visit, routine repair. Alex attaches the invoice to the *P1 Visitor Garage Door* asset history and closes it out.


* **Scenario 2 (The Major Project & Sub-Projects):** Two years later, the garage door mechanism fails completely due to age. The Board decides to do a complete $60,000 overhaul of the entire garage entry system, including structural concrete repairs around the frame and installing new high-speed rubber doors.
* **Alex’s Decision:** **Major Project with Sub-Projects.**
* **Main Project:** *2026 Underground Garage Entrance Overhaul*
* **Sub-Project 1:** *TNR High-Speed Door Installation Contract* (Contractor A)
* **Sub-Project 2:** *Concrete Portal Frame Restoration* (Engineering Firm + Masonry Contractor B)





---

#### Example B: The Elevator System

* **Scenario 1 (The Task):** Passenger Elevator #2 stops working on Tuesday because a door sensor was misaligned. Otis Elevator arrives, resets the sensor, and leaves a service ticket.
* **Alex’s Decision:** **Task.** Logged under *Elevator #2* maintenance history.


* **Scenario 2 (The Major Project):** The elevators reach their 25-year lifespan. The Board hires an elevator consultant, receives three RFP bids, and approves a $350,000 multi-year modernization campaign.
* **Alex’s Decision:** **Major Project with Sub-Projects.**
* **Main Project:** *2026-2027 Elevator Modernization Campaign*
* **Sub-Project 1:** *Cab Interior Redesign & Lighting Upgrade*
* **Sub-Project 2:** *Mechanical Hoist & Control Board Replacement*

### Summary Checklist for Board Review

| Category | What Is It? | Who Does It Involve? | How Long Does It Last? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Task** | A quick repair or routine service call. | 1 Vendor, standard operating invoice. | 1–3 days. |
| **Major Project** | A multi-step, high-dollar building overhaul or improvement. | Board, Property Manager, Engineers, Competing Contractors. | Weeks to Months. |
| **Sub-Project** | A distinct, major component or contract nested inside a Major Project. | Specific trade vendor assigned to one phase of a larger job. | Bound by the phase timeline. |
